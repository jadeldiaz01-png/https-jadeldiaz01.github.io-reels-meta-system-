# CineForge Multi-Video Reference Analysis

## Scope

Ten user-supplied short videos were analyzed as a quality and storytelling reference set. They are not source footage for reproduction. CineForge may learn abstract production traits only.

## Measured common technical signature

- 10/10 at 30 fps.
- 10/10 HEVC source, yuv420p, BT.709 primaries/transfer.
- 10/10 AAC stereo at 44.1 kHz.
- Dominant source frame is 720x1280 vertical; one source is 720x720 and one is 720x836.
- Duration range: 10.546-22.207 s; median 15.366 s.
- Visual motion varies widely, so the system must not enforce one motion level or one editing tempo.
- Median highlight clipping is extremely low in the sampled frames; exposure protection is a useful quality trait.

Delivery is intentionally upgraded to 1080x1920 minimum, 30 fps, BT.709 SDR, H.264 High Profile, AAC 48 kHz, faststart.

## Observed creative families

### Physical risk / real-world action

The viewer is shown the spatial setup before the decisive movement. The camera preserves geography so trajectory, landing/contact and consequence are easy to read. The lesson is not to imitate the stunt; it is to preserve spatial causality and hold the outcome.

### Animal expression / behavior

The animal is introduced quickly and the strongest value comes from readable posture, gaze or facial expression. A medium-to-close progression works because the reaction itself is the payoff. CineForge should prioritize identity, eyes, fur/feather detail and natural micro-motion rather than exaggerated anthropomorphic morphing.

### Human reaction

Cause precedes reaction. The strongest frames are usually the emotional response after an interaction rather than the setup itself. CineForge should hold the post-event reaction long enough to register and preserve eyelines and identity.

### Transformation / reveal

The viewer understands a before-state, anticipates a change, and receives a visually legible reveal. Match geometry and object persistence matter more than flashy transitions.

### Sports/action payoff

A wide enough frame preserves biomechanics and target/trajectory visibility. Contact physics and completion matter more than aggressive reframing or speed ramps.

### Comedic fail / reversal

A normal expectation is established, then a reversal occurs. The edit stays simple enough that the cause of the failure is understandable in one viewing. The consequence/reaction is part of the joke.

## Shared design principles adopted by CineForge

1. One clear event beats several weak events.
2. Hook means an unresolved visual question, not necessarily text.
3. Preserve setup before payoff.
4. Hold reaction/consequence after payoff.
5. Prefer readable subject and environment over gratuitous shallow depth of field.
6. Camera movement must have narrative motivation.
7. Natural motion and contact physics are higher priority than stylized speed manipulation.
8. Use foreground/midground/background when geography matters.
9. Protect highlights and shadow detail; creative grading comes after shot normalization.
10. Text is optional and secondary to visual comprehension.
11. Watermarks, creator handles, exact source identity, dialogue and source audio are excluded from generated masters.
12. Reference material never relaxes originality, rights, safety or platform-policy gates.

## Model-routing implications

- Animal/recurring character: require reference consistency and image-to-video/reference support.
- Complex physical action: require high temporal coherence and motion control.
- Camera-led reveal: require explicit camera control.
- Human dialogue/reaction: require identity stability and lip-sync only when speech is necessary.
- Simple observational scene: do not force expensive high-motion or cinematic-camera models if a cheaper model reaches the same QC floor.
- No model meeting the floor => BLOCKED; never silent downgrade.

## Platform implications

Meta's 2026 originality guidance makes copying or low-value editing of third-party clips strategically harmful. The reference set therefore informs abstract hooks, pacing, visual causality and quality gates only. Every produced concept must be original and independently generated/filmed.
